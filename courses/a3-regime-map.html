The regime map, and which one touches me

Three big rulebooks come up again and again in any governance conversation: Singapore's PDPA, the EU's GDPR, and the EU AI Act. Part 0

A map, not a deep dive

You do not need to read three rulebooks to govern well. You need to know which ones are in the room and roughly what each is for - the same way you can navigate a city with a subway map without knowing every street. This session draws that map. The deeper courses handle the how; today is about the shape and the reach.

Part 1 · covers the three regimes

Three regimes at a glance 8 min live

Here are the three, each in one line. Singapore's PDPA is the baseline for handling personal data in Singapore (plus the Do Not Call rules for marketing). The EU's GDPR is the world's most influential privacy law, built around the rights of people in the EU. The EU AI Act is the first big law aimed squarely at AI systems, sorting them by how risky their use is. Read the table across, not down.

LiveSingapore's PDPA - the home baseline3 min▶

The Personal Data Protection Act is Singapore's baseline for handling personal data. If your organisation collects, uses, or discloses data about people in Singapore - and almost every one does, if only staff records - the PDPA's core promises apply: notice, consent, purpose limitation, protection, access and correction, and telling people when a serious breach happens. Bundled alongside it are the Do Not Call (DNC) rules, which govern marketing to Singapore phone numbers. Think of the PDPA as the floor you are almost certainly standing on already.

LiveThe EU's GDPR - the influential one3 min▶

The General Data Protection Regulation is the EU's privacy law and the template much of the world now copies. It covers personal data much as the PDPA does, but with stronger, more detailed rights (including the erasure and portability we met in Session 2) and heavier duties - a lawful basis for every use, and reporting serious breaches fast. The headline for a Singapore reader is its reach: the GDPR protects people in the EU, wherever the organisation sits. We unpack that in Part 2.

LiveThe EU AI Act - the new one for AI2 min▶

The EU AI Act is the first major law written specifically for AI systems. Instead of treating all AI the same, it sorts uses by risk: a few uses are banned, some "high-risk" uses (like AI in hiring or credit decisions) carry heavy duties, and most everyday AI carries light or no extra obligations. It is about what the AI does to people - its output and effect - which is why, like the GDPR, it can reach beyond Europe. You do not need the tiers memorised today; just know it exists and that risk is its organising idea.

Part 2 · covers extraterritorial reach

The laws follow the person, not your office 7 min live

Here is the idea that surprises most founders: a European law can bind a company that has never set foot in Europe. The GDPR protects people in the EU - so if you offer goods or services to them, or track their behaviour, you are in scope even from a desk in Singapore. The EU AI Act works the same way for AI: if your system's output is used to make decisions about people in the EU, it reaches you. The PDPA is more homebound - it is about your handling of Singapore people's data. The rule to remember: these laws follow the person (or the AI's effect), not your address.

LiveWhy the GDPR can reach Singapore3 min▶

The GDPR sets out its own reach (this is Article 3, and that is the only number worth knowing today): it applies not just to organisations based in the EU, but to any organisation, anywhere, that offers goods or services to people in the EU or monitors their behaviour. So a Singapore online shop that ships to Berlin, prices in euros, or runs analytics on EU visitors can be in scope. It is not about a signup for every foreign visitor who stumbles across your site - it is about whether you are aiming at or tracking people in the EU. When in doubt, that is a "get advice" moment.

LiveHow the EU AI Act reaches you2 min▶

The EU AI Act follows the AI's effect. If you build or use an AI system whose output is used to make decisions about people in the EU - screening EU job applicants, scoring EU customers - the Act can apply, even if your servers and staff are entirely in Singapore. The obligations scale with the risk tier of that use, but the reach principle is the same as the GDPR's: it follows the people affected, not the flag on your building.

Real world

The Singapore SaaS that grew into scope. A small Singapore analytics startup sold only locally - PDPA, and that was that. Then it landed a customer in the Netherlands and started processing that customer's EU users' data. Overnight the GDPR was in the picture, not because the startup moved, but because the people it now touched were in the EU. Nothing about the office changed; the reach did. That is exactly the pattern this session trains you to spot early.

Self-studyWhat "in scope" does and does not mean2 min read▶

Being "in scope" of a law means its duties can apply to you - not that you are automatically in trouble. It is the signal to learn that regime's basics and, for anything material, get proper advice. Two calming truths: first, most Singapore small businesses live mainly under the PDPA and only meet the GDPR when they deliberately go after EU customers. Second, scope is about facts you control - who you sell to, whom you track, where your AI's decisions land - so you can often choose your exposure. The chooser in Part 3 is built to surface those facts, not to scare you.

Exercise 2 of 2

Three questions that set your scope ★ 8 min · your next team chat

These are the same facts the chooser turns on. Get your team to answer them out loud and you will know your governance footprint.

EU people: "Do we offer goods or services to, or track the behaviour of, anyone in the EU?" A yes brings the GDPR into the room.

AI on people: "Do we use AI whose output helps decide something about people - especially people in the EU?" A yes puts the EU AI Act on the watch list.

SG marketing: "Do we call, text, or fax marketing to Singapore numbers?" A yes means the Do Not Call rules sit on top of the PDPA.

The mindset You are not trying to become a lawyer. You are learning to notice when a new customer, a new market, or a new AI feature quietly changes which laws apply to you - so the answer is "we already know", not "we had no idea".
