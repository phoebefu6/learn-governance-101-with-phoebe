Part 0

You have the words and the map - now the plan

Five sessions ago, governance was a wall of acronyms. Now it is a mental model: personal data is information that points to a person, you govern it for trust, risk, and law, and a handful of regimes set the rules depending on who you serve and what you build. That is genuinely most of the battle. What is left is not more theory - it is deciding where to go deeper and taking the first small action. This session hands you both: a shelf of four deeper courses, and a five-step starting plan you can begin before the week is out.

Read this first This whole course has been educational, not legal advice. The deeper courses go further into the actual obligations, but for a real program you will still want to check specifics against the official sources and, where it matters, a professional. Think of yourself as now able to ask the right questions - not to have every final answer.

Part 2 · covers your starting plan

Your five-step starting plan 8 min live

You do not need a big project to begin. You need five small, ordered steps - each one takes an afternoon at most, and each one makes the next easier. Do them in order. By the end you will have a real map of your data, a person who owns it, and a clear first fix underway.

LiveStep 1 - make a one-page data register2 min▶

Before anything else, write down what you hold. One page, three columns: what (the kind of data - email list, HR records, CCTV), where (which system or drive it lives in), and why (the reason you have it). You built the start of this in Session 1. This single page is the foundation every deeper course assumes you have.

Real world

The register that took 40 minutes. A small clinic thought a data register meant a big software project. Instead the office manager listed nine things on one sheet in under an hour - and immediately spotted two old spreadsheets nobody remembered, full of patient contact details, sitting on a personal laptop. The register did not just document the problem; finding it was the first fix.

LiveStep 2 - name a DPO or an owner2 min▶

Governance with no owner quietly dies. Name one person who is accountable - a DPO if your situation calls for one, or simply "the person who looks after data here" for a small team. It does not have to be a lawyer or a full-time role to start. It has to be a real name, so that when a question or a problem appears, everyone knows who picks it up. Session 5 covered what this role actually does.

LiveStep 3 - run the "which regime touches me?" chooser2 min▶

Now that you know what data you hold and who serves whom, run the chooser (it is right below, in Part 3). It asks a few plain questions - do you handle Singapore personal data, do you serve people in the EU, do you build AI that affects people - and points you at the regimes that apply. This tells you which deeper course to take, so you are not guessing.

LiveSteps 4 and 5 - go deeper, then fix your biggest gap2 min▶

Step 4: take the deeper course the chooser pointed you to. Reading it while you have a real data register in hand makes it land far harder than theory alone.

Step 5: fix your single biggest gap first - do not try to fix everything. For most beginners the biggest gap is one of two things: collecting more than you need (stop asking for it), or a sensitive dataset with no protection (lock it down). Pick the one that would hurt most if it went wrong, and start there.

One gap, done, beats ten gaps noted A finished small fix builds momentum and trust. A long list of unfixed problems just builds dread. Close one, then choose the next.

Exercise 1 of 2

Choose your next course and your first action ★ 10 min · write it down

This is the exercise that makes the whole course count. Two sentences, written down, with dates. That is it.

Based on the chooser, name the one deeper course you will take next. Write it as a commitment: "I will start [course] by [date]."

From your data register, pick your single biggest gap - the thing most likely to hurt if it went wrong. Write one first action: "This week I will [do the specific thing]."

Put both sentences somewhere you will see them - a sticky note, a calendar event, a message to yourself. A plan you cannot find is a plan you will not do.

Exercise 2 of 2

Three questions to close the loop ★ 6 min · reflect

Answer these out loud or on paper. They gather everything the six sessions gave you into three sentences you can act on.

Which regime touched you? After the chooser - is it PDPA, GDPR, the EU AI Act, or a combination? Name it plainly.

What is your biggest gap? Looking at your register, where are you most exposed - too much collected, or something sensitive left unprotected?

Who owns it? Who, by name, is now accountable for closing that gap and for the data going forward? If the answer is still "nobody", that is your very first action.

The mindset you leave with You do not need to know everything about the law to govern data well. You need to know what you hold, why it matters, which rules touch you, and who is looking after it. You now know how to find all four. That is a genuinely strong place to stand.
Homework

Your first week ◐ start this week

  • Open (or enrol in) the deeper course you chose - just open the first session this week, so it is real and not "someday".
  • Start your one-page data register if you have not already: what you hold, where it lives, why you have it. Aim for done, not perfect.
  • Confirm who owns data protection where you are - and if the answer is nobody, raise it with the person who can decide.
  • Pick your single biggest gap and write down one action to close it. Do that action, or book the time to.
